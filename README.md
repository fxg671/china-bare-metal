## The Connectivity Problem Nobody Talks About Enough

Most hosting providers treat "Asia" as one homogeneous market. It's not.

Getting low-latency, stable connectivity into mainland China from a server in Hong Kong or Los Angeles isn't automatic. The Great Firewall isn't just a content filtering system — it's a physical network reality that affects routing, packet loss, and latency for anyone trying to reach Chinese users from outside.

The providers that figured this out early — and built infrastructure around it — are in a completely different category than everyone else.

The dominant local players like Alibaba Cloud, Tencent Cloud, and Huawei Cloud have their own advantages: they operate from within mainland China, they understand compliance requirements, and they have massive infrastructure. Alibaba holds about 36% market share, Huawei around 16%, Tencent around 9%.

But here's what those providers don't do well: **international connectivity**. If your business needs to serve both mainland Chinese users *and* a global audience — if you're running a cross-border SaaS, a game with international players, a financial platform with global transactions — you're stuck choosing between mainland-optimized and international-optimized.

This part tends to get abstract in most infrastructure discussions, so let me make it concrete.
